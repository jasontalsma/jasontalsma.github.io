This is a redesign of a demo unit designed to passively sense breath alcohol content (BAC) and display the results in one integrated, portable package.

The first design required a laptop connected on the side, making for an awkward setup to put on display. The president of the R&D company ([KEA Technologies)](https://jasontalsma.wordpress.com/about-jason-experience/) requested an entirely self-sustaining module to take on the road for show booths and customers. My role was to design and fabricate an attachment for a CPU with a monitor and integrate a power source.

![DemoSensor.gif](images/e2026-87d8fb_9f368db73bff40328d0fbccc53093e1dmv2.gif)

The addition of a Windows OS tablet required a major redesign with an incredibly short lead time. Additions of vertical arms fixed on either side on the back and a sleeve for the tablet enable complete system portability. Ease of use was prioritized in mechanical functionalities as it could be utilized by uninformed users.

The design constraints included an independent power source, multiple viewing angles, compact, and lightweight. I took a rapid prototyping approach to ensure real-world fidelity as soon as possible. Four revisions were created in SolidWorks before achieving the final model printed using in-house FDM.

The outputs from most of these models were used to solidify much of my additive manufacturing fundamentals through the iterations seen below.

![3d printed hinge](images/5421d-87d8fb_1b4d3b5768254905ba57352a3962fc2amv2_d_2815_5201_s_4_2.jpg)

![Optimized\_for\_printing.jpg](images/0708f-87d8fb_126916d889f742d48967960dbf09ea18mv2_d_4730_2764_s_4_2.jpg)

Note the flat sides for optimal printing and absence of support structure. Optimization for additive manufacturing was thoroughly practiced throughout the design. I also continued to hone other design for additive manufacturing techniques, principles, and intuition for printed mechanical components.

![](images/image-1.png)

Multiple viewing angles of the sensor outputs was achieved with a notched hinge with its axle mounted in a 45° angled slot in order to back out the screen to disengage the hinge and pack away the device.

The entire system required a case as it was to be flown in check-in luggage for multiple flights. I designed a sturdy case with the minimal time remaining making sure to maximize the printer rastering and infills. 

I had to deal with a shortage of orange filament while printing the case, however I easily edited the exact same SolidWorks file to create an even better press fit cap for the end. This was a simple yet powerful rediscovery of the agility of additive manufacturing.

![Failed\_Print.jpg](images/d5238-87d8fb_b6f8c12d3a31418998abd76308edf36amv2_d_2984_3814_s_4_2.jpg)

I created every piece of this project to fit to an existing bespoke chemical-electrical system in less than two weeks. The unit was carried for use in Australia and performed beautifully (...when plugged into 120V.  Which was a very valuable lesson).
