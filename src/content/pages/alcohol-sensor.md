_The goal of this project was to create a portable unit to demonstrate an automotive passive breath alcohol content (BAC) sensor and display the results in one integrated package._

The first design required a laptop connected on the side, making for an awkward setup to put on display. The president of the R&D company KEA Technologies requested an entirely self-sustaining module to take on the road for show booths and customers. My role was to design and fabricate the demo unit combining a tablet, on-board power source, and the chemical-electrical sensor system itself.

![Design Layout 3](/images/35404-87d8fb_191762b0d466476eb35642467f99fdefmv2_d_5312_2988_s_4_2.jpg)

The design constraints included an independent power source, multiple viewing angles, compact, and lightweight. I took a rapid prototyping approach to ensure real-world fidelity as soon as possible. Optimization for additive manufacturing was thoroughly practiced throughout the design. I also continued to hone other design for additive manufacturing techniques, principles, and intuition for printed mechanical components.

Multiple viewing angles of the sensor outputs was achieved with a ratcheted hinge.

![multiple angles](/images/image-1.png)

The system required a case as it was to be flown to sites. I designed a sturdy case with the minimal time remaining making sure to maximize the printer rastering and infills. A shortage of orange filament mid-print lead to a simple yet powerful rediscovery of the agility of additive manufacturing.

I created every piece of this project to fit to an existing bespoke chemical-electrical system in less than two weeks. The unit was first used in Australia and performed beautifully (...when plugged into 120V. Which was a very valuable lesson).
