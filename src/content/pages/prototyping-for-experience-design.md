### Shop Tools

Common shop tools were used to create a sliding clothing rack to minimize the disorganization recently worn clothing causes in my bedroom. This design organizes the experience you have with a door.

![](images/9c32d-87d8fb_bbc6a25f3b75495cb7205a3948873e85mv2_d_4116_2738_s_4_2.jpg)

My idea was to organize clothing that I do not want to hang up or put in the hamper upon entry to my bedroom door. This subset of my clothing is a constant nuisance and continually obstructs useable space by my front door. I wanted to create a more streamlined and more fun experience of walking into my front door.

![](images/4fe0d-87d8fb_d9a8fca84d1a43308ea621e2d309c09cmv2_d_4585_1315_s_2.jpg)

I created a mechanical device to accept thrown clothing and make them more compact in some way. Many initial designs included triggered hooks to move the clothes into a corner of the room, out of the way. The final design was mounted flat along the wall beside a front door. Three arms were fitted with pegs for hanging clothing.

![](images/04d4e-87d8fb_2f4ee1dfffff4e0d8ca433dde275c1a9mv2_d_5312_2988_s_4_2.jpg)

![](images/51698-87d8fb_6ecc14c6ade74f2f812bfc4870171badmv2_d_5312_2988_s_4_2.jpg)

![](images/3a3c6-87d8fb_4edbebb614054c40a14e6153a6afaed3mv2_d_5312_2988_s_4_2.jpg)

This wood worked project was a vertical slice of the front door organization experience. The form and function made it a succesful looks-like and works-like prototype.

The next steps of this design would be to gamify and enhance the experience of throwing clothes onto the rack. This would be accomplished by adding LEDs to the different pegs and/or a pivot for a balancing feature.

### Laser Cutting

Laser cutting was used to create an attachable mouse-pad that also acts as a protective case during transport. This design organizes an activity I experience daily.

![](images/ea36c-87d8fb_c599c238424246c1aae6b2277608d119mv2_d_5312_2988_s_4_2.jpg)

The laser cut design I decided to go with was an attempt to solve the clutter brought about by not using a mouse on a table. I came to this idea from my personal experiences using CAD and trying to juggle a laptop, calculator, mouse, and a notebook as a makeshift surface. Having to carry the extra flat surface around with my laptop also clutters the experience of heavy mouse use with a laptop. I am often doing work on my own or a friend’s couch and further narrowed my design scope with that in mind.

![](images/efdf5-87d8fb_d0c276a918a5444489eb32eefade2c01mv2.png)

My goal with this project was to create a laser cut mouse pad prototype that could attach to a laptop. The design is intended for those who are heavy users of a mouse pointer device with a portable computer. I intended to create a deployable system that articulates to offer a flexible flat surface that is extended from the main computer body when in use.

This 1/8 in. laser cut plywood artifact is a works-like prototype wherein it can actually perform the unfolding and provide an adequate surface as well as pack away as intended.

The next steps of my design would be to add a better method of attaching one’s mouse to the contraption and providing another degree of freedom to allow for even more diverse situations. The mouse pad should be able to bend or hinge in the axis lateral to the hinges in the current design to allow for optimal flat surface placement on the go.

### Additive Manufacturing

Fusion deposition modelling 3D printing techniques were leveraged to create a combination of 3 tools, a money clip, Bic lighter holder, and a bottle opener in one. This design increases the functionality of each tool and overall experience due to their combination.

<figure>

![](images/2b47c-87d8fb_14e668aefe594fbcb311ebfea83668abmv2_d_4078_2719_s_4_2.jpg)

<figcaption>

Combine tools to enhance functionality using additive manufacturing.

</figcaption>

</figure>

This 3D modeling and 3D printing project inspired a more curvaceous design to solve the given prompt. Said prompt for this project was to combine two tools to increase their usefulness. I attempted to improve the experience of using the tools required to spend money. The tools are a money clip, a lighter, and a bottle opener. The money clip organizes one’s paper and plastic currencies so that he/she can spend easily and efficiently. The other two tools come into play after the money is so easily spent. The bottle opener opens drinkable vices and the lighter ignites inhalable ones; both are often used as painkillers for all the money that was just spent. This design increases their usefulness by improving their proximity to each other, and therefore, their accessibility.

https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/combined-tools-7faefbeb61b040d2a123e2dba7370002

I created a works-like and looks-like prototype, or a vertical slice, of the experience. The model was a rather accurate visual representation of the theoretical final product and could perform all its functions (money and lighter holding as well as bottle opening) but not to the degree or level of fidelity the final product would have to achieve. I utilized SolidWorks for digital model creation and a MakerBot FDM 3D printer for the fabrication of the digital file.

The future steps for the design would include perfecting the material choice for clip retention and lifespan, slimming down the overall shape to a more compact design, and optimizing the bottle opener. These steps would be achieved through digital testing of materials and forms for durability as well as physical user testing to make further decisions on materials and form for usability. I would also like to conduct user testing to see if it even improves the experience at all.

### Arduino

Arduino microprocessor and C based code were leveraged to create a system for ensuring you never forget to put anything back into your bag on the go. This design senses if something is in the right place and gives feedback that it is.

![](images/20190402_110715-ezgif.com-optimize.gif)

I'm always concerned about forgetting my wallet in restaurants, passport in the airport, or my camera on a bench. International travel especially makes me want some way to tell if all the important items I'm carrying are in the right spot and to tell me that they are. I designed a system to count your items on the way out of the bag and the way back into the bag to check if something has been left out using a photo resistor and a vibration sensor with a stepper motor to close the zipper after use. I approached the idea trying to think of many different forms of feedback but kept coming back to the idea that it should be as unobtrusive as possible. The less conscious the interaction, the more pleasurable the interaction will be.

- ![](images/35476-87d8fb_cf3bc32462174eacbf10e92f34ade573mv2_d_4726_2835_s_4_2.jpg)
    
- ![](images/c084b-87d8fb_47d3cb9a66024f4fa649d305a29ddc8dmv2_d_5312_2988_s_4_2.jpg)
    
- ![](images/3b7a9-87d8fb_f7dcc7fd82b94f7fba19ca028bb18bbamv2_d_5312_2988_s_4_2.jpg)
    

For this project, I created a system to ensure you remember to put everything back in your book bag after an impromptu study session, a quick café visit, or maybe a hectic stand up meeting. A light sensor mounted to the lip of a book bag’s opening counts the number of items taken out of the bag. When you need to head out, the system will count how many items you put back into your bag. If you do not put as many items back as you took out, the system will alert you and will not close the zipper that you are making use of. If the same number of items are put back into your bag as were taken out, the system will give the correct feedback and will automatically close the bag’s zipper when hoisted onto your back by way of a vibration sensor and stepper motor. The vibration sensor will trigger the stepper motor to run in one direction enough to completely close the zipper then in the opposite direction to reset the actuator (so you can open it again later).

For example, imagine showing up late to an exam. You whip out your notebook, your binder, a pencil, and a calculator. After sweating through the exam, you pack up to rush on to the next midterm. You put back your notebook, your binder, and the pencil. As you go to close your book bag, the alert LEDs flash!  You forgot your calculator! You toss the last item in your bag, LEDs give an all accounted for signal, and the zipper closes itself as the bag is slung on your shoulder and hustle to your final exam.

This design is a functional prototype used to convey the experience this system would foster.

Future steps for this project would include implementation of a higher torque stepper motor, a permanent mounting system, streamlined code, lower profile components, and better integrated components.

### Augmented Reality

Augmented reality applications were used to restructure an existing room by changing the items in the space. This design organizes the experience I have with a room.

For this project, I edited a space to reduce clutter and improve usage. I decided to video capture an indoor eating area facing a full window. I added a new table and seat arrangement to allow for easier movement around the eating area and window. Furthermore, I added a couple lounge chairs facing the natural light source in the room.

I created a looks-like prototype where the user would be able to see what the new room experience would be like through augmented reality devices.

![](images/artg_ar_before-ezgif.com-video-to-gif-converter.gif)

![](images/artg_ar_after-ezgif.com-video-to-gif-converter-1.gif)

Some changes to this design would include the use of a different space to better showcase the changes made. I would also make sure to show a clearer before and after.

### Virtual Reality

The Unity game engine was used to create a virtual reality representation of a reused outdoor quad space at my university. This design changes the experience of a space that could be better utilized than it currently is.

I approached this project thinking it would be impossible in such short a time, however,  the use of prefab components and others’ shared creations opened my eyes to modularity and accessibility of the technology to the average consumer. Centennial quad on Northeastern University’s campus was chosen due to its large size and actual feasibility as a space for such an experience.

For this project, I created a virtual reality environment to depict a possible experience to create in Centennial Quad at Northeastern University. The experience is a multi-sensory overload for users. There is a ball pit fountain, a meatball cart, a seesaw, mountain bike tracks, and of course, giant swings.

I created a vertical slice prototype of the experience.
